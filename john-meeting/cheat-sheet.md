# Meeting Cheat Sheet — John & Roxann

## The 3 things to remember under pressure
1. **Sequence discipline** — listen → understand → ask → identify opportunity → propose prototype. Never lead with "here's what you should build."
2. **Calibrated confidence** — they're the industry experts. You're the AI/execution expert. Stay in that lane.
3. **Concrete close, not a big close** — the win is one specific next step ("let me prototype this outside your production system"), not a signed contract tomorrow.

## 10 questions to ask once he shows you the platforms
1. Of everything you've built, which platform is closest to actual launch, and who's the first real user it's built for?
2. Walk me through the single workflow your users do most often today — where does it currently break down or slow down?
3. What proprietary data or content have you and Roxann accumulated over 30 years that isn't in the software yet?
4. If you could fix one thing about the current user experience, what would it be?
5. How is the platform going to make money — subscription, commission, lead gen, supplier membership, something else?
6. What's the current tech stack, and how is your development team structured?
7. Where do your planners or suppliers get frustrated enough that you hear about it directly?
8. What content or data changes constantly, and what's essentially static once entered?
9. If AI could solve exactly one problem in the platform in the next 90 days, what would move the needle most?
10. Where do you feel like you're missing expertise on your team right now, as you head toward launch?

## Likely questions from John — quick answers

**"What's your actual AI/coding background?"**
> Not a traditional software engineer — background is entrepreneurship, sales, business (websites, e-commerce, NFC review cards). Gone deep on AI-assisted development recently: Claude Code, modern tools, rapid prototyping. Edge is combining business judgment with these tools to move from idea to working prototype fast — not knowing every line of code from memory.

**"Can you actually build something real, or is AI doing the work for you?"**
> AI writes a lot of the code, but I decide what to build, structure it, test it, and know when the output is wrong. That's a real skill most people can't do yet. Everything in the demo tonight, I built and can explain.

**"What is Jota Agency?"**
> My business — practical AI and automation for businesses: websites, lead capture, AI customer communication, automation workflows. Jota 24/7 is one product inside it — always-on lead response and qualification. Early-stage, but real and hands-on.

**"Security / APIs / integrations — do you know what you're doing there?"**
> Work with APIs regularly, follow standard practices (auth, not exposing secrets, input validation). Not claiming enterprise-security-team depth. On anything touching production or customer data, would want to work carefully with your existing dev team rather than assume I know your constraints.

**"What would you charge / what relationship are you looking for?"**
> Not trying to price anything tonight. Rather earn it — prototype one real opportunity outside your production system, you see if it's valuable, then we talk about what a working relationship looks like.

**"What could you contribute that your existing dev team can't?"**
> Probably not lower-level engineering depth — your team's been building this for years. What I bring: fast AI-specific prototyping and a business lens on where AI creates real value vs. noise. Complementary to your team, not a replacement.

## Don't say
"AI revolution" · "10x everything" · "replace your employees" · "replace your developers" · "fully autonomous company" · anything implying you already know how their platform works before they show you.

## If you get an NDA
Sign it, then say less about anything you're shown until you understand the terms. Don't reference or speculate about their product outside the room afterward.
