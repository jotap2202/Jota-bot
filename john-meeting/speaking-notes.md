# Speaking Notes — John Meeting

Tone: practical, curious, business-oriented. Not a lecture. Aim for ~7 minutes on the deck, then stop talking and let him lead.

## Opening (before slide 1)
"Thanks for having me — I kept this short on purpose. I'd rather spend most of our time on what you've built than on my slides."

## Slide 1 — Title
"I wanted to keep this short — most of our time should go to your platform, not my slides."

## Slide 2 — What You've Already Built
"I'm not walking in assuming you need to start over. You've already done the hard part — the domain expertise and the working product. The question I'm curious about is where AI can multiply the value of what's already there."

## Slide 3 — Where AI Can Add Value
"These are the five places AI tends to matter in a platform like this. I don't know yet which of these matters most for you — that's what I want to learn today."

## Slide 4 — From Search to Conversation
"This is one pattern I think is interesting for your planners — not replacing your data, just changing how someone gets into it."

## Slide 5 — AI Meeting & Event Concierge (Concept)
"I built a small standalone prototype to make this concrete rather than theoretical. I'll show it live in a few minutes — it's disconnected from any real system, just a thinking exercise."

## Slide 6 — Turning 30 Years of Knowledge Into a Digital Advantage
"The part I find most interesting isn't the chat interface — it's that you have decades of judgment that's currently in your heads and your team's heads. There are real, careful ways to make parts of that queryable through software. That's a knowledge-architecture problem, not a 'upload it to ChatGPT' problem."

## Slide 7 — AI Agents & Automation
"This is the more operational side — less flashy, but often where the real time and revenue is sitting."

## Slide 8 — Human + AI
"I want to be clear about the boundary — I'm not proposing automating your judgment or your relationships. Those are the actual asset."

## Slide 9 — What I'd Explore First
"That's my whole pitch. I'd rather spend the next 45 minutes on your platform than on my slides — can you show me what you've got?"

---

## Transition into the live demo
"Before we look at your platform, let me show you the small prototype I mentioned — takes two minutes."

- Open the demo (`localhost:4173`), point out the "concept demonstration — not connected to any real system" label out loud.
- Type or read the example request live.
- Narrate what's happening: "This is calling the model live right now, structuring the request into fields, and generating one follow-up question — nothing here is scripted."
- Close it out: "The interesting part isn't this screen — it's that this same pattern could sit in front of whatever data and workflows you've already built."

## Transition into their platform
"Now I'd love to see what you've built — walk me through whatever makes the most sense to start with."

Then: **listen → understand → ask → identify → (only if it comes up naturally) propose the prototype step.**

## Behavioral reminders (say these to yourself before the meeting, not out loud)
1. Don't prescribe fixes before you've seen the platform.
2. Stay in your lane: AI/execution, not 30 years of events expertise.
3. End goal: one concrete next step, not a closed deal.
